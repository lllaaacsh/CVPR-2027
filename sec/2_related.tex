\subsection{World action models}
\label{sec:related_wam}
    World models capture environment dynamics to support latent imagination~\cite{ha2018worldmodels,hafner2020dreamer,hafner2023dreamerv3}, visual planning~\cite{finn2017foresight,zhou2024dinowm,assran2025vjepa2}, and interactive simulation~\cite{yang2024unisim,bruce2024genie,jang2025dreamgen}.
    Recent world action models (WAMs)~\cite{pai2025mimicvideo,ye2026dreamzero} build on pre-trained video foundation models~\cite{nvidia2025cosmos,wan2025wan} to provide physical priors beyond static vision-language-action models (VLAs)~\cite{brohan2023rt1,brohan2023rt2,kim2024openvla,black2025pi0}.
    Early WAMs synthesize future frames or subgoal images and infer actions via inverse dynamics or goal-conditioned policies~\cite{du2023unipi,ko2024avdc,black2024susie,tian2024seer,zhao2025cotvla}, while related models generate video frames and actions autoregressively~\cite{wu2024gr1,cheang2024gr2}.
    Diffusion-based WAMs either jointly denoise video and action tokens in unified or dual-expert transformers~\cite{guo2024pad,li2025uva,zhu2025uwm,kim2026cosmospolicy,ye2026dreamzero,bi2026motus,li2026lingbotva}, or condition a separate action decoder on internal video features~\cite{wen2024vidman,hu2025vpp,liao2025ge,pai2025mimicvideo,ma2026dit4dit,zhang2026deva}.
    To reduce multi-step rollout latency, recent methods omit test-time imagination~\cite{zheng2025flare,yuan2026fastwam} or extract future features in a single forward pass~\cite{pai2025mimicvideo,ma2026dit4dit,zhang2026rift}.
    Even in single-pass WAMs, however, the video backbone processes all future tokens at uniform full resolution, leaving dense spatiotemporal computation unaddressed.

% World models (WM) that predict how the environment evolves have been used to support policy learning in imagination~\cite{ha2018worldmodels,hafner2020dreamer,hafner2023dreamerv3}, predictive planning ~\cite{finn2017foresight,zhou2024dinowm,assran2025vjepa2}, and data generation~\cite{yang2024unisim,bruce2024genie,jang2025dreamgen}. Building on the WMs, world action models (WAMs) transfer priors from pretrained video generation models~\cite{nvidia2025cosmos,wan2025wan} to robot policies~\cite{ye2026dreamzero,pai2025mimicvideo}, complementing vision-language-action models (VLAs) built on vision-language models~\cite{brohan2023rt2,kim2024openvla,black2025pi0}.

% Existing WAMs differ mainly in how actions are obtained from the video generation model. Prior work on WAMs utilizes generated future frames or subgoal images to infer robot actions by inverse dynamics or goal-conditioned policies~\cite{du2023unipi,ko2024avdc,black2024susie,tian2024seer,zhao2025cotvla}. These methods pay for generating the future at every decision. Future prediction can also supervise policies that act from the current observation at test time~\cite{yuan2026fastwam},
% similar to related approaches that are trained to anticipate embeddings of future observations~\cite{zheng2025flare}. Other methods jointly predict future frames and actions~\cite{cheang2024gr2,wu2024gr1}. Other diffusion-based approaches achieve the joint prediction by denoising video and actions with either a single transformer~\cite{guo2024pad,kim2026cosmospolicy,li2025uva,ye2026dreamzero,zhu2025uwm} or coupled video and action experts~\cite{bi2026motus,li2026lingbotva}. In this work, we study WAMs that use separate action modules to read the video model's internal representation of this prediction~\cite{hu2025vpp,wen2024vidman,liao2025ge,pai2025mimicvideo,ma2026dit4dit}. This interface lets us examine which parts of the future representation require detailed computation for action prediction.



\subsection{Diffusion acceleration and token reduction}
\label{sec:related_future}
    Accelerating dense transformer backbones has been widely studied in video generation and visual recognition.
    Video diffusion accelerators reduce computation through feature caching~\cite{liu2025teacache,zhao2025pab}, spatiotemporal sparse attention~\cite{xi2025sparsevideogen}, and region-adaptive sampling~\cite{liu2026ras}.
    In vision and vision-language transformers, training-free token reduction merges visually similar tokens~\cite{bolya2023tome,bolya2023tomesd,li2024vidtome,hyun2025sttm} or prunes tokens with low attention scores~\cite{liang2022evit,chen2024fastv,zhang2025sparsevlm}.
    Other architectures allocate variable compute via mixed-resolution patches~\cite{ronen2023mixedres} or lightweight token selectors trained end-to-end~\cite{ryoo2021tokenlearner,rao2021dynamicvit,raposo2024mod}.
    However, diffusion accelerators and visual heuristics optimize perceptual quality or appearance redundancy, which often diverge from control relevance in robotic manipulation.
    

% Generating future frames is time-consuming and unsuitable for robot tasks that require efficiency; therefore, Efficient WAM design aims to reduce the cost of representing or predicting the future. $\Delta$-IRIS tokenizes changes between frames~\cite{micheli2024deltairis}, mimic-video extracts future features in a single forward pass~\cite{pai2025mimicvideo}, RIFT replaces iterative rollout with learned anticipation tokens~\cite{zhang2026rift}, and Fast-WAM removes future prediction at test time~\cite{yuan2026fastwam}.
% General diffusion acceleration uses feature caching~\cite{liu2025teacache,zhao2025pab}, sparse attention~\cite{xi2025sparsevideogen}, and region-adaptive sampling~\cite{liu2026ras} to reduce cost while preserving generation quality, and DiT4DiT conditions an action transformer on the features of a video transformer trained together with it~\cite{ma2026dit4dit}. At the same time, general diffusion acceleration methods can also be applied, such as feature caching~\cite{liu2025teacache,zhao2025pab}, sparse attention~\cite{xi2025sparsevideogen}, and region-adaptive sampling~\cite{liu2026ras} to reduce cost while preserving generation quality.



\subsection{Action-aware compression for embodied control}
\label{sec:related_tokens}
    Focusing representations on task-relevant dynamics is a central goal in decision-aware world models based on value equivalence~\cite{grimm2020valueequivalence,schrittwieser2020muzero} and distractor separation~\cite{fu2021tia,wang2022denoisedmdp,pan2022isodream}. 
    Compact visuomotor policies similarly predict frame deltas~\cite{micheli2024deltairis}, point tracks~\cite{wen2024atm,bharadhwaj2024track2act}, or object flow~\cite{xu2024im2flow2act} instead of dense video, yet these approaches train specialized models from scratch rather than using frozen video backbones.
    Within pre-trained VLAs, learned token compression uses object-centric tokenization~\cite{bendikas2025oatvla}, instruction-driven routing~\cite{li2025cogvla}, or differentiable pruning~\cite{brohan2023rt1,jiang2025lightvla}, all of which update backbone parameters.
    Training-free VLA accelerators keep the backbone fixed and prune observation tokens via instruction attention~\cite{yang2025efficientvla}, temporal caching~\cite{xu2025vlacache,tan2025flashvla}, speculative scheduling~\cite{li2025spvla,wang2026specprune}, motion gates~\cite{pei2026adp}, or historical action attention~\cite{liu2025vlapruner}.
    For WAMs, Sparse-WAM~\cite{xie2026sparsewam} and CF-Prune~\cite{kim2026cfprune} score future tokens by action and instruction attention at an initial full-resolution step and prune later denoising steps.
    In single-pass WAMs, however, decoder-attention pruning requires an extra full-resolution pass, while hard token deletion removes coarse workspace layout and breaks contiguous temporal indexing.
    Different from prior work, \method supervises a shallow router solely with action-decoder gradients inside a frozen video backbone, and coarsens low-relevance regions across space and time without token deletion.

Other methods combine semantic attention with action-decoding attention from recent control steps~\cite{liu2025vlapruner} or learn a selector while fine-tuning the policy~\cite{jiang2025lightvla}. Some training-free methods extend selection to the future tokens of WAMs: they score tokens by action attention~\cite{xie2026sparsewam} or by action and instruction attention~\cite{kim2026cfprune} at the first denoising step and compute only the selected tokens in the remaining steps.
