\subsection{World Action Models}
\label{sec:related_wam}

Learning to predict how the environment evolves has long served robot control.
World models have been used to train policies in imagination~\cite{ha2018worldmodels,hafner2020dreamer,hafner2023dreamerv3},
to plan by predicting the outcomes of candidate actions~\cite{finn2017foresight,zhou2024dinowm,assran2025vjepa2},
and as interactive simulators and data generators~\cite{yang2024unisim,bruce2024genie,jang2025dreamgen}.
Video generation models trained on web-scale video~\cite{nvidia2025cosmos,wan2025wan} now provide such predictions with strong visual and physical priors,
and WAMs turn them into policies that output robot actions~\cite{ye2026dreamzero,pai2025mimicvideo}.
WAMs thereby complement vision-language-action models (VLAs), which build policies on vision-language models~\cite{brohan2023rt2,kim2024openvla,black2025pi0}.

Existing WAMs differ mainly in how the action is obtained from the video model, which also determines what the video model must compute at inference.
\emph{Explicit-future} WAMs generate the future as images or video and act on it:
actions are inferred from generated frames or subgoal images by an inverse dynamics model or a goal-conditioned policy~\cite{du2023unipi,ko2024avdc,black2024susie,tian2024seer,zhao2025cotvla},
or predicted together with the future frames~\cite{wu2024gr1,cheang2024gr2}.
They pay for generating the future at every decision.
\emph{Observation-conditioned} WAMs use video prediction only as a training signal and act from the current observation at test time~\cite{yuan2026fastwam},
similar to policies trained to anticipate embeddings of future observations~\cite{zheng2025flare}.
\emph{Joint} WAMs denoise video and actions together in one network,
either a single transformer that processes observation, future and action tokens~\cite{guo2024pad,li2025uva,zhu2025uwm,kim2026cosmospolicy,ye2026dreamzero}
or a mixture of transformers whose video and action experts interact through joint attention~\cite{li2026lingbotva,bi2026motus}.
\emph{Future-conditioned} WAMs keep the two apart:
the video model predicts the future, and a separate action module reads the video model's internal representation of this prediction~\cite{hu2025vpp,wen2024vidman,liao2025ge,pai2025mimicvideo,ma2026dit4dit,zhang2026deva}.
The categories overlap, since some joint models also generate future frames and their action tokens attend to future tokens as well;
what separates joint from future-conditioned WAMs is whether video and action are computed in one network or in a cascade.

\subsection{Future-Conditioned World Action Models}
\label{sec:related_future}

We focus on future-conditioned WAMs.
Keeping the action decoder separate lets the video model be trained for prediction alone, while a comparatively light decoder learns to read it~\cite{hu2025vpp,liao2025ge,pai2025mimicvideo}.
The decoder typically reads intermediate features rather than pixels.
VPP conditions an implicit inverse dynamics model on the future representation inside a video diffusion model~\cite{hu2025vpp},
VidMan turns a video diffusion model pretrained on robot videos into an inverse dynamics model through a layer-wise adapter~\cite{wen2024vidman},
mimic-video cross-attends to an intermediate layer and finds that a single forward pass, without denoising the future frames, already gives strong performance~\cite{pai2025mimicvideo},
DeVA reads several layers through a separate action expert~\cite{zhang2026deva},
and DiT4DiT conditions an action transformer on the features of a video transformer trained together with it~\cite{ma2026dit4dit}.
Much of the cost of these policies lies in the video model, and existing work reduces it by shortening the generation process:
by running fewer denoising steps, down to a single forward pass~\cite{pai2025mimicvideo},
by building the future representation in one pass with learned anticipation tokens instead of an iterative rollout~\cite{zhang2026rift},
or by not predicting the future at test time at all~\cite{yuan2026fastwam}.
General accelerations of diffusion models, through feature caching~\cite{zhao2025pab,liu2025teacache}, sparse attention~\cite{xi2025sparsevideogen}, token merging~\cite{bolya2023tomesd,li2024vidtome} or region-adaptive sampling~\cite{liu2026ras},
aim to preserve the generated images or video, a goal that a policy does not share.
In all of these approaches, each forward pass still computes the full token grid of the future at the resolution of video generation, whichever parts the action decoder relies on.
We address this complementary axis:
within a single forward pass, our router decides at which resolution each region of the predicted future is computed, and it measures relevance through the action decoder that reads them.

\subsection{Action-Aware Token Selection}
\label{sec:related_tokens}

Token reduction accelerates transformers by merging similar tokens~\cite{bolya2023tome,bolya2023tomesd,li2024vidtome,hyun2025sttm}
or by dropping tokens that receive little attention~\cite{liang2022evit,chen2024fastv,zhang2025sparsevlm}.
Learned variants train a small network with the task loss to sparsify tokens~\cite{rao2021dynamicvit}, to summarize them into a few~\cite{ryoo2021tokenlearner}, or to route them past blocks~\cite{raposo2024mod},
and mixed-resolution tokenization processes less salient regions at a lower resolution~\cite{ronen2023mixedres}.
In robot learning, RT-1 compresses its image tokens with a TokenLearner trained end-to-end with the action loss~\cite{brohan2023rt1,ryoo2021tokenlearner}.
VLAs have adopted these tools to cut the cost of their visual tokens.
VLA-Cache reuses tokens that change little between steps while recomputing task-relevant ones~\cite{xu2025vlacache},
EfficientVLA keeps tokens that attend to the instruction together with a diverse complement~\cite{yang2025efficientvla},
FlashVLA reuses actions across stable steps and prunes low-contribution tokens~\cite{tan2025flashvla},
SP-VLA pairs spatio-semantic token pruning with action-aware model scheduling~\cite{li2025spvla},
CogVLA aggregates and sparsifies tokens according to the instruction~\cite{li2025cogvla},
and Oat-VLA represents the scene with a few object- and agent-centric tokens~\cite{bendikas2025oatvla}.
Several recent methods make the selection explicitly action-aware:
ADP gates text-driven pruning by the recent end-effector trajectory~\cite{pei2026adp},
SpecPrune-VLA adjusts how aggressively it prunes by whether the current action is coarse or fine, judged by the speed of the end effector~\cite{wang2026specprune},
VLA-Pruner adds the attention of the action-decoding stage to that of the vision-language prefill~\cite{liu2025vlapruner},
and LightVLA learns differentiable token pruning by fine-tuning the policy with its task loss~\cite{jiang2025lightvla}.

Our router differs from these methods in purpose, signal, operation and architecture.
They accelerate a policy and use relevance to decide what can be dropped with little loss;
we seek the part of the predicted future that the action relies on, and the reduction in computation follows from it.
Their relevance is a proxy, such as attention to the instruction or from action tokens, change between steps or end-effector motion, or it is learned while the whole policy is fine-tuned;
our router is supervised only by the gradient of the action decoder's objective, whose main term measures how far the action moves from the one predicted with the full representation,
while the video model and the decoder stay frozen apart from a low-rank adapter.
They discard tokens or reuse stale ones, whereas we keep every region and change only the resolution at which it is computed,
merging spatial neighborhoods and temporal segments with position handling suited to a generative video backbone, so a coarse view of the whole scene remains available to the action decoder.
Allocating resolution or computation across regions has also been driven by visual saliency~\cite{ronen2023mixedres} or by the model's focus during generation~\cite{liu2026ras}; our criterion is the action instead.
Finally, these methods operate on the observation tokens of a vision-language model,
whereas we operate on the predicted-future tokens inside a video diffusion transformer, after one of its early blocks and upstream of a separate action decoder,
so the savings apply to the computation of the video model itself.
