\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced ARROW, a framework that reduces the cost of world action models by allocating spatiotemporal resolution according to action relevance. An early router learns from the action decoder to guide spatial merging and temporal aggregation while retaining scene coverage. Velocity distillation adapts the decoder to the compressed context with the video backbone frozen. Experiments on two WAM backbones demonstrate lower inference cost, including a \(1.59\times\) speedup on mimic-video with 20\% of its future tokens. On six real-robot tasks, ARROW achieves 77.8\% success and a \(1.84\times\) speedup over the native policy.

Our current formulation uses a predefined total token budget, and real-robot evaluation is limited to one platform. Future work will explore scene-dependent budgets and broader validation on longer-horizon tasks and additional platforms.
