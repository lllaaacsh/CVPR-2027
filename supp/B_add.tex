    In \mainref{tab:main_results}, VLA-Pruner~\cite{liu2025vlapruner} and ADP~\cite{pei2026adp} exceed the nominal compressed token budgets due to full-context warm-start or motion-gated fallback queries.
    Here we evaluate all baseline selection rules under strict fixed token budgets on LIBERO across three evaluation seeds.

\paragraph{Fixed-budget evaluation on mimic-video.}
    \Cref{tab:supp_fixed_mimic} restricts every policy query on mimic-video to 4,080, 5,760, or 7,440 total tokens, including the 2,400 observation tokens.
    VLA-sel.\ substitutes compressed instruction-relevance queries for the three full-context warm-start queries per episode to initialize the attention history.
    ADP-sel.\ disables the end-effector motion gate and all full-context fallback queries, while CF-sel.\ retains the initial full-context probe pass.
    Without full-context fallbacks, ADP-sel.\ drops to 24.9\%, 51.3\%, and 62.7\% mean success across the 10\%, 20\%, and 30\% budgets, and VLA-sel.\ drops to 51.3\%, 73.7\%, and 82.1\%.
    In contrast, \method achieves 79.8\%, 90.5\%, and 92.9\% mean success at matched single-pass FLOPs and latency, whereas CF-sel.\ reaches lower accuracy at more than twice the latency.

\paragraph{Routing layer depth}
    \Cref{tab:supp_depth} varies the video DiT block where routing occurs on LIBERO-Spatial at the 10\% future-token budget.
    By default, \method routes after block 2 of the 20 DiT blocks, leaving 18 blocks to process the compressed 4,080-token sequence.
    Block 1 is the earliest layer where the router observes transformer block features rather than raw patch embeddings.
    Routing after block 1 reduces latency from 404.2\,ms to 394\,ms, but lowers success rate by 12.4\% from 69.2\% to 56.8\%.
    A single DiT block therefore provides insufficient spatiotemporal context for action-relevance prediction.
    Routing deeper after block 6 raises success rate to 78.2\% at 4,080 tokens while increasing latency to 445\,ms.
    At a comparable latency of 440.7\,ms in \mainref{tab:main_results}, routing after block 2 at the 20\% budget achieves 88.2\% success.
    Retaining 1,680 additional future tokens after block 2 is thus more effective than running four extra blocks at full resolution.
    Routing beyond block 9 at the 10\% budget exceeds the 477.1\,ms latency of block 2 at 30\%, which already surpasses Full-context at 92.0\% versus 90.4\%.

\paragraph{Router and distillation objectives.}
    The upper half of \Cref{tab:supp_objectives} ablates the Stage-1 router objective and Stage-2 decoder objective on LIBERO-Spatial at the 10\% budget.
    Retraining the Stage-1 router with binary cross-entropy at $\gamma=0$ instead of focal loss lowers success rate by 9.2 pp from 69.4\% to 60.2\%.
    Removing $\mathcal{L}_{\text{budget}}$ at $\lambda_b=0$ or $\mathcal{L}_{\text{entropy}}$ at $\lambda_e=0$ lowers success rate by 3.2\% and 3.6\%, respectively.
    Omitting pseudo-labels so the router trains solely on straight-through gradients from \Cref{eq:supp_ste} plus regularizers reduces success rate by 20.6\% to 48.8\%.
    For Stage-2 decoder adaptation, velocity distillation alone at $\lambda_{\text{GT}}=0$ reaches 69.6\%, closely matching 70.8\% under the full task objective.
    In contrast, ground-truth flow matching without teacher distillation fails at 0.0\%, confirming that full-context distillation is essential for compressed-context adaptation.

\paragraph{Relevance score sources.}
    The lower half of \Cref{tab:supp_objectives} substitutes alternative spatial scores for the learned group scores $S_g$ under the identical two-stage allocation rule.
    On LIBERO-Spatial, ranking groups by block-2 visual self-attention lowers success rate by 5.8\% at the 10\% budget and 3.6\% at the 20\% budget.
    Using a fixed random spatial map causes larger drops of 12.8\% at 10\% and 16.8\% at 20\%.
    Substituting the \emph{Training-mean map} (scores averaged over training demonstrations) at 20\% lowers success rate by 4.6\% on LIBERO-Spatial, 7.8\% on LIBERO-Goal, and 4.4\% on LIBERO-Object.
    Input-conditioned per-query routing is therefore required across varying object layouts and instructions.

\paragraph{Spatial and temporal budget allocation.}
    \Cref{tab:supp_split} evaluates how a fixed future-token budget is distributed between spatial merging and temporal pooling on LIBERO-Spatial.
    Temporal-only pooling at $F=300$ retains all 1,200 spatial tubes and lowers success rate by 2.6\% at 10\%, 10.0\% at 20\%, and 2.6\% at 30\%.
    Keeping all 1,200 spatial tubes restricts each tube to 1 or 2 segments at 10\% and 2 or 3 segments at 20\%, which over-compresses motion dynamics.
    Conversely, spatial-only merging at $K=14$ leaves at least 4,200 future tokens when all 300 groups are merged.
    This variant therefore applies only at the 30\% budget with $F=20$ for 360 tubes, lowering success rate by 5.8\% from 92.0\% to 86.2\%.
    At the 20\% budget, rescaling temporal tiers to 90/165/195 tubes with $K=5/7/9$ at $F=50$ and 150/270/330 tubes with $K=2/4/6$ at $F=150$ preserves 3,360 future tokens.
    These settings reach 87.6\% and 85.0\% success, compared with 88.2\% at our default $F=100$.

\paragraph{Temporal priority and segment boundaries.}
    \Cref{tab:supp_temporal} fixes spatial group selection and ablates temporal tier assignment and segment partitioning.
    On LIBERO-Spatial, the \emph{Feature change only} variant ($u_p=\Delta_p$) lowers success rate by 3.4\% at 10\% and 1.6\% at 20\%.
    Reversing tube priority to $-u_p$ lowers success rate by 5.4\% at 10\% and 5.0\% at 20\%.
    The \emph{Flat counts} variant, which assigns near-uniform segment counts via a fixed spatial pattern, reduces success rate by 4.0\% at 10\% and 0.6\% at 20\%.
    Randomizing tier assignments at the 20\% budget lowers success rate by 2.4\% on LIBERO-Spatial, 4.0\% on LIBERO-Goal, and 4.6\% on LIBERO-Object.
    Finally, substituting equal-length segments for the variation-balanced boundaries in \Cref{eq:supp_cuts} reduces success rate by 4.6\% at 10\% and 4.4\% at 20\%.



\paragraph{Task-agnostic spatial criteria.}
    \Cref{fig:supp_relevance}a compares the \method router against five task-agnostic criteria over the 300 spatial $2\times 2$ groups.
    Let $z^{(2)}_{t,p}$ denote the full-context feature after video block 2 at future frame $t$ and spatial position $p$ prior to coarsening, where higher group scores retain full resolution.
    \emph{Similarity} computes the negative mean pairwise cosine similarity among the four tokens in a group across the 14 future frames to favor less redundant regions.
    \emph{Motion} averages $1-\cos(z^{(2)}_{t,p},z^{(2)}_{t+1,p})$ across adjacent future frames and the four positions of each group.
    \emph{Block-2 self-attention} averages the attention weights received from all visual queries across heads, the four group positions, and the 14 future frames.
    \emph{Edge energy} applies horizontal and vertical Sobel filters to the channel-mean grayscale final RGB observation and averages gradient magnitudes within each group region.
    \emph{Random} draws an independent, reproducibly seeded uniform score in $[0,1]$ per group for each window.

\paragraph{Offline coarsening protocol.}
    Across 200 held-out windows from 50 LIBERO-Spatial episodes, each criterion ranks the 300 groups once and retains the top-$n$ groups at full resolution for $n\in\{25,50,100,150,200,250\}$.
    Remaining groups are averaged spatially at top-left source coordinates across all 14 future frames to isolate spatial coarsening.
    All criteria share identical observations, video and action noise draws, and the unadapted base action decoder.
    Action distortion is computed as the geometric mean across windows of the normalized action MSE over the first five predicted steps relative to Full-context.

\paragraph{Insertion curves and ranking reversal.}
    As shown in \Cref{fig:supp_relevance}a, router-guided selection achieves the lowest action distortion across 50 to 250 full-resolution groups among all compared criteria.
    Reversing the router scores to $1-S_g$ yields the highest offline action distortion across all group counts in \Cref{fig:supp_relevance}a.
    In closed-loop evaluation in \Cref{fig:supp_relevance}b, score reversal reduces success rate from 70.8\% to 28.4\% at 10\%, from 88.2\% to 60.2\% at 20\%, and from 92.0\% to 73.2\% at 30\% even with a retrained LoRA adapter.

\subsection{Coverage analysis}
\label{app:supp_diagnostics}



\paragraph{Semantic region coverage across suites.}
    \Cref{tab:supp_region_coverage} reports the percentage of ground-truth simulator pixels per semantic category inside the top 100 of 300 spatial groups across LIBERO-Spatial, LIBERO-Goal, and LIBERO-Object.
    All five selection rules score identical frames from 50 recorded episodes per suite under the 20\% \method policy, where uniform random selection gives 33.3\%.
    Queries 0 to 2 of each episode are excluded so VLA-Pruner initializes a three-step attention history.
    Across the three suites, \method covers 84.3\% to 99.9\% of robot pixels, 90.1\% to 97.1\% of task-object pixels, and 77.0\% to 96.7\% of future-path pixels.
    Notably, \method achieves the highest future-path coverage on every suite while selecting far fewer distractor-object pixels than VLA-Pruner and CF-Prune, such as 37.7\% versus 90.9\% and 97.0\% on LIBERO-Object.
